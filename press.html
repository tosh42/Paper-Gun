Two hundred words

Paul Tosh is a singer, pianist and looping artist based in Manheim, Pennsylvania, where he also works as a supervisor on a farm. Born in Scotland, the son of two opera enthusiasts, he trained with the classical voice coach Lide DeRosa in Glasgow and learned his trade in piano bars across Europe before settling in the United States.

For roughly two decades he has performed in senior residences, churches, synagogues, community centres and theatres across the United States and Canada — several thousand performances, and several thousand conversations afterwards. Among the residents he has sung to: a backing singer for Ray Charles, a pilot who flew for Mariah Carey, a chef who cooked for Bill Gates, and a man who worked security for Bill Clinton.

Out of those rooms came Paper Gun, an original operetta in three acts for one performer and a piano, in which a single character is sung by two voices — the man, and the voice in his head that keeps the argument going. It is about resentment and what to do with it, and it is used by presenters as a shared therapeutic experience as much as a concert. He also performs The Song Is The Play, in which songs by Dylan, Sinatra, the Beatles and others are acted out as theatre with live piano looping. He tours in a Cybertruck fitted with a custom camper and wrapped in lyrics from his own songs.
