Paper Gun · After the Performance

What Comes
After

The spoken frame that closes the evening
Paul Tosh  ·  tosh42.com  ·  bookings@tosh42.com

This is what follows a two-hour operetta. It is not a replacement for one. The show takes you from resentment to grief. What to do next is also important. The talk back also covers that. Ten minutes, fifteen at most.

It is the same frame every time, which is the point: a presenter can know in advance exactly what their people will be sent home with, rather than hoping. What follows is it, in full.

01 · The Starting Point

Sadness is a doorway, not an end

Grief is better than resentment, and that is a starting point for what a future can hold in many positive ways. The question is what comes after the success of being sad as opposed to angry.

02 · The Two Precursors

Building anything has two precursors

Be believable — to yourself first, and then you will be believable to others. Not to convince anybody of anything, not to trick yourself, not to make excuses for yourself. And to not blame — not yourself, not others. If you are someone who blames, you cannot build. If you are someone who is not believable to yourself, you cannot build. Any building process requires those two very, very basic things.

03 · The Precondition

But neither can be attained without satisfaction first

All of that is good and well and can be agreed with, and it is entirely useless information without prior satisfaction. The reason people blame is that they are filling in for not being satisfied. Satisfaction is the first goal, not the last.

04 · Where Satisfaction Comes From

And satisfaction comes from learning, for its own sake

It does not have to be a spiritual practice. It can be any practice, anything you learn — crochet, model-building, anything at all — but it has to be for its own sake, something you actually like, not something you want to capitalise on. Get better at it because you like doing it. That is the cornerstone, and it is a lifelong process.

05 · Why the Show Comes First

None of which is available while you are resentful

Which is where the show comes back in. The only way out of being resentful is to grieve, which is what the whole piece is about. There are phases to that, which is another discussion; I’ve got a printout of all that stuff, how that works, and a person can work with a therapist and go through the steps of how you get past it. And there are timelines, and there’s reasonable achievements, levels of achievement where you can reasonably say you can attack another stage.

For Presenters

What this means for your room

The show does the feeling. This gives people somewhere to put it.

People leave a performance moved and with nowhere to take it. Fifteen minutes later they leave with something to do on Monday. That is usually the part that makes the evening programmable rather than just bookable.

In clinical and therapeutic settings it runs longer, and it is co-held

Twenty to thirty minutes with a clinical partner, reflective rather than confessional, with the facilitator guiding depth. The frame above still opens it. See the full care frame →

The transcript is available in advance

If you want to brief a clinical partner, or simply decide whether it suits your room, the full transcript of the talkback can be sent before you book.
