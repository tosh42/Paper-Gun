What this is

A standalone evening drawn from a larger work that a congregation can take: the lament, the plea, the prayer and the release. It is not the operetta cut down. It is a different program using some of the songs for a faith organization.

It is a set about carrying something you cannot put down, and what to do with it. It is a sermon subject, not a therapy subject.

The running order

This Is Mine opens and closes it, which is how the full work is built too — the same four lines meaning something different by the end.

  • 1This Is Mine — The offer. Four lines. Sets the terms before a word of explanation.
  • 2Fear Hate — The warning that names everybody’s faith at once.
  • 3You and Your Own — Why it is the nearest and dearest we withhold from.
  • 4The Open Secret — The first time the defending voice admits what the defending costs.
  • 5The Journey — Two people on the same road, travelling apart.
  • 6A Little Amnesty — The plea. Asking for less than forgiveness.
  • 7Broken Man — The raw plea.
  • 8The Space Between — The prayer in the eye of the storm.
  • 9Phenomenal Heart — Let it out and bring it to the surface. The turn.
  • 10Let Me Cry — The release. The closest thing here to a lament.
  • 11Your Paper Heart — You don’t have to tell me why.
  • 12Faith — An answer rather than as the subject being changed.
  • 13For the Narrator — For the one who stayed through all of it. Dear Lord, give me wisdom.
  • 14Coming Home — Seasonal, and optional.
  • 15This Is Mine — The close. Same four lines. Now they mean what they say.

An optional talkback

The show is catharsis. The talkback is where the work starts. The method at the back of the program came out of a relationship that couldn’t be repaired.

Ten minutes at the end, for anyone who wants to stay. It is the same frame every time, so a presenter knows in advance what their people are sent home with: that sadness is a doorway rather than an end, that nothing gets built by someone who blames, but that satisfaction as a precursor comes from learning for its own sake.
