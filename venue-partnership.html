For Venues

Two ways to host the show

Whoever brings the audience takes the bigger share. Pick the arrangement that fits how you like to work — both are on this page, and both are simple.

Option A · Simplest for you

You provide the room.
I fill it.

80 / 20Performer / Venue

  • You supply the room, a piano, and someone on the door
  • I do all promotion — posters, street, socials, my own audience list
  • I carry every cost and every risk
  • Ticketing runs through my box office; you are paid after the show
  • Your bar and food takings are entirely yours
◻ We'll take Option A

Option B · We fill it together

You promote to your people.
We share it.

60 / 40Performer / Venue

  • Everything in Option A, plus your promotion
  • One email to your mailing list, no later than two weeks before
  • At least one social post, plus a poster displayed in the venue
  • Listing on your website or newsletter events page
  • If the promotion doesn't go out, the split reverts to Option A
◻ We'll take Option B

Every seat filled is a bar tab, a dinner, and a room that feels alive. The show is one hour; the evening is yours.

Recommended Pricing

A two-tier structure, kept deliberately simple. One price everywhere — online, at the door, and in person. Seats are limited by the room, and that scarcity is part of the offer.

Standard

General Admission

$35Per seat

  • One hour — one man, one piano, every song built live
  • Same price online, on the street, and at the door
  • Never discounted; a thin room is filled with complimentary seats, not price cuts

Limited · Twelve only

Patron Seat

$60Per seat

  • Reserved seating
  • A signed Patron Edition of the script — the full operetta, all three acts, with the author's notes
  • A conversation with the performer after the show
  • Capped at twelve, and the cap is real

Venues are welcome to propose a different price for their room — but the tiers stay in this shape, and the standard ticket is never publicly discounted.

The Split

◻Option A — 80 / 20 in the performer's favour. Venue provides room, piano, and door staff.
◻Option B — 60 / 40 in the performer's favour, conditional on the promotion listed above being carried out.

The performer receives the greater of   (minimum) or the agreed percentage of net ticket revenue.

"Net" means gross ticket sales less ticketing and card-processing fees (approximately $1 per ticket). Bar, food, and merchandise revenue are excluded from the split and belong entirely to the venue.

Insurance

The Venue maintains general liability insurance covering its premises. The Performer maintains general liability insurance of not less than $1,000,000 per occurrence and will supply a certificate of insurance naming the Venue as additional insured on request. Each party is responsible for its own equipment and property.

If the Show Cannot Go Ahead

Either party may cancel in writing no later than fourteen days before the performance without penalty. Inside fourteen days, the parties will make reasonable efforts to reschedule before cancelling. Neither party is liable for events beyond reasonable control.
